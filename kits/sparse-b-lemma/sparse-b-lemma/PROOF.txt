Sparse B's forced half-modulus difference

Assume two word sequences obey the SHA-256 message expansion, with word width
w and arithmetic modulo q=2^w. All words outside the stated support agree:
  differing input words: 8,9,10,11,13,15
  differing expanded rows through W39: 16,20,25,28,29.
The stated support is EXACT: W13 must differ, not merely be allowed to differ.

Define d_t=W_t(B)-W_t(A) modulo q. The exact recurrence gives

W20 = sigma1(W18) + W13 + sigma0(W5)  + W4
W29 = sigma1(W27) + W22 + sigma0(W14) + W13
W36 = sigma1(W34) + W29 + sigma0(W21) + W20.

The supports imply that W18,W5,W4,W27,W22,W14,W34,W21,W36 agree. Equal
inputs give equal sigma outputs. Subtracting the two versions of each equation
therefore yields d20=d13, d29=d13, and 0=d36=d29+d20=2*d13 modulo q.
This uses common-addend cancellation, NOT sigma applied to an additive delta.

For the representative integer d in [0,q), 2*d is divisible by q exactly at
d=0 and d=q/2. W13 differs, excluding zero. Hence
  d13=d20=d29=q/2=2^(w-1).
Adding q/2 toggles only the highest bit. Thus each of the XOR differences at
W13,W20,W29 is the top bit alone: 80000000 at width 32.

No u/n direction is fixed: either side can have that bit set. This statement
also holds for odd widths; it cannot imply an even-width-only existence law.
If the support is only an allowed set and W13 may agree, the zero root remains
and the forced nonzero XOR conclusion cannot be imposed unconditionally.

This is a necessary implication, not existence of a pair satisfying the whole
schedule. It says nothing about a compatible compression trajectory, initial
state law, collision, trial probability, attack cost or contest eligibility.

Scope of the program: exhaustive roots at widths 1..12 and finite local
common-addend controls through width 32. These are not complete schedule
witnesses. The universal argument is the proof above, not the finite sample.
